M Continuous Idea to MVP / Field Guide
Founder intent + evidence + creative synthesis

No internal approval gates

Build the idea the founder actually means.

Start with a fragment or a fully formed vision. Preserve the founder's product, feature, interaction, visual, business, and technical ideas; then connect them to evidence, creative product thinking, and a focused delivery loop that produces a testable MVP.

The central shift

This is not a one-way stage machine. Research, product definition, design, architecture, implementation, and verification continuously inform each other through one living workbook.

Operating principle

Creativity completes the idea. Evidence keeps it honest.

Agents may make well-reasoned product leaps to connect founder intent into a complete business and product direction. Each leap is recorded as an inference or assumption, tied to its rationale, and given a way to test or revise it.

  • Preserve intent and record why a material direction changes.
  • Let research revise scope, UX, positioning, architecture, and delivery immediately.
  • Keep actual evidence distinct from plans, estimates, and agent reasoning.
  • Use the smallest next test or implementation slice that resolves the highest-risk uncertainty.

Core endpoint

A tested MVP package, not a forced launch.

The workflow reaches its core outcome when the product is coherent, traceable, and tested enough for a deliberate next decision. Beta, public launch, and operations can follow, but they do not block internal MVP work.

  • Founder intent and product thesis remain visible.
  • Evidence, assumptions, and risks are distinguishable.
  • Scope, journey, narrative, and measurement are explicit.
  • Architecture, build traceability, and actual test evidence are recorded.
  • Known issues and required external authorizations are clear.
!

Authorization protects real-world effects, not internal progress.

Internal research, synthesis, design, code, review, and revision proceed continuously. Explicit founder authorization is required only before an action affects people, money, systems, data, or public claims outside the workspace.

  • outreach and interviews
  • publishing and public claims
  • purchases, domains, and paid services
  • credentials, DNS, and production changes
  • personal or sensitive data handling

What a Codex task should do

Read the idea brief, workbook, evidence, decisions, relevant app materials, the matching local skill, and the mapped AAS playbooks. Then choose the highest-leverage unresolved question or delivery slice.

  • Update relevant workbook sections, not a serial handoff packet.
  • Preserve prior founder direction and explain material changes.
  • Label facts, inferences, and assumptions.
  • Record actual evidence and tests, never imagined results.
  • End with next work and any external authorization needed.
How changes stay coherent

The Direction Log explains what changed and why. The Scope Changes section ensures new ideas stay meaningful without silently expanding the MVP. The Risk Register and Evidence Ledger make uncertainty visible.

  • Research changes that affect scope, positioning, or risk update the MVP definition.
  • Technical discoveries that affect cost, trust, or feasibility update the product definition.
  • Founder's new ideas enter as durable inputs, then receive a confidence label and next step.
  • Optional launch work remains separate from MVP completion.
