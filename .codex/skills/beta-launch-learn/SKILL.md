# Beta, Launch, and Learning

## Objective

Learn whether the product creates repeatable value and whether acquisition can become sustainable, before scaling spend or scope.

## Metric Definitions

Define metrics before interpreting them:

- Acquisition: qualified visitors, leads, and signups.
- Activation: users completing the first value event.
- Engagement: users repeating the core value action.
- Retention: cohort-based return/use over a defined period.
- Revenue: trials, paid conversion, MRR, ARPA, refunds, churn.
- Support: ticket volume, response time, recurring issue classes.
- Reliability: errors, incidents, latency, uptime, provider failures.

Always include cohort, denominator, date range, source, and limitations.

## Beta Procedure

1. Invite a narrow ideal-customer cohort only after founder approval.
2. Observe onboarding and first value.
3. Pair behavior data with qualitative calls or written feedback.
4. Triage bugs affecting trust, data, payment, activation, and retention first.
5. Review findings weekly.
6. Turn recurring evidence into the next smallest product or distribution experiment.

## Launch Procedure

Choose one or two channels. For each specify:

- Audience and offer.
- Message and asset.
- Owner and approval status.
- Budget cap.
- Attribution and events.
- Support and incident plan.
- Success/failure threshold.

## Decision Rules

- Scale when retention, value evidence, and channel economics are improving.
- Optimize when problem/value is clear but activation, onboarding, or conversion is weak.
- Pivot when repeated evidence contradicts the segment, problem, wedge, or model.
- Pause/kill when demand, retention, economics, or risk cannot justify further investment.

## External-Action Boundary

Do not send invitations, customer messages, campaigns, ads, refunds, or public announcements without explicit founder approval. Do not claim outcomes without sourced actual data.

## Output

Create `beta_learning_packet`, `launch_report_packet`, or `operating_review_packet` with actual data, findings, decision options, and the next experiment.
