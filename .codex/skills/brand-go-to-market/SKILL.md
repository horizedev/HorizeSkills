# Brand and Go-to-Market

## Objective

Translate validated customer insight into clear, differentiated, truthful product communication and a focused route to early users.

## Positioning Formula

```text
For [target customer] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [primary outcome].
Unlike [main alternative], it [credible differentiator].
```

Use the customers' language when possible. Do not make positioning broader than the evidence.

## Messaging Hierarchy

1. Outcome headline.
2. Who it is for and the triggering context.
3. How it works at a high level.
4. Evidence or proof, only if verified.
5. Clear call to action.
6. Objection handling: cost, time, trust, switching, and fit.

## Brand Direction

Define a practical starter system:

- Position and promise.
- Tone and prohibited claims.
- Product naming candidates and rationale.
- Color, typography, visual motif, and accessibility constraints.
- Terminology glossary.

## Launch Channel Evaluation

Rank channels by:

- Where the ideal customer already pays attention.
- Founder credibility and access.
- Speed to feedback.
- Cost and effort.
- Measurability.
- Likelihood of high-intent users rather than vanity reach.

Choose one or two channels for the first launch.

## External-Action Boundary

All naming choices, domains, public copy, price publication, emails, ads, testimonials, community posts, and outreach need founder approval. Create drafts and experiments, not unapproved public artifacts.

## Output
